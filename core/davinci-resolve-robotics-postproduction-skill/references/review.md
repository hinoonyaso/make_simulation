# Post-production review

Use this after the first complete preview, not before assembly.

## Pass 1 — Story
- Is the central question obvious early?
- Does every beat causally lead to the next?
- Can any beat be removed without losing understanding? If yes, consider removing it.

## Pass 2 — Visual
- One dominant attention target per beat.
- Avoid editor-added zoom when source animation already guides attention.
- Check text safe areas, equation dwell time, occlusion, and Manim/Blender style continuity.

## Pass 3 — Motion
- Remove dead air that adds no thinking time.
- Preserve deliberate pauses where the viewer must inspect geometry/equations.
- Avoid simultaneous camera move + text reveal + graph change unless intentionally staged.

## Pass 4 — Audio
- Narration is always understandable.
- SFX are sparse and semantically motivated.
- BGM supports pacing but disappears perceptually during difficult explanations.

## Pass 5 — Technical
- Correct project/timeline frame rate and resolution.
- No offline media, gaps, accidental black frames, clipped audio, or duplicate subtitles.
- Validate render configuration before queueing final output.

## Reported equation-section reading pressure

Inspect the final export where a viewer must read captions, locate component values and follow an equation transition. A technically timed caption can still compete with the active visual task. Sequence the focus: source → correspondence → settled relation/result. Retain meaningful holds and shorten repeated outcome prose when needed. Return insufficient measured speech/duration to Director and narration; new utterances require refreshed timing/captions, and moving tokens require renderer changes. Do not fix reading pressure by globally slowing the export, trimming speech or adding music. Record the interval and whether the repair was actually watched/listened to at normal speed.

## Preserve a worked example through finishing

At changed representation cuts, verify the same run/state remains identifiable through part, geometry, command and response. For an intended continuation, compare the last and first visible source times, root pose, target and a shared landmark: freeze/match the state before resuming when it helps orientation. If the next shot restarts an earlier interval, explain the replay; do not make it look like the next chronological response. A dissolve or run-name label alone does not establish state correspondence. Inspect the final composited frames on both sides of the cut. A stopping replay has its own experiment/time label; it must not look like the next state of the avoidance run. Preserve render-only annotation disclosures and ideal-geometry labels. Retiming must retain source order and documented source-to-presentation mapping.

Check the first experiment establishes orientation; later cases emphasize changed evidence, rather than repeating setup by habit. Composition may change by task while speech remains intact. Do not impose a duration/cut quota, trim accepted narration or fill spare time with decorative effects. Review actual final captions with the common-center/radius derivation, altered-span comparison and feedback handoff; retain enough time for the active inference. Playback/listening is required for rhythm claims, separate from frame checks.

When local narration pauses or complete-sentence cuts change beat length, rebuild the concatenated narration and sentence cues from the declared edits. Verify untouched utterances against the approved source and record edited boundaries/fades. Compare revisions at matched beat-relative explanatory states, with each version's global timestamp recorded separately; identical global timestamps cease to be matched evidence after a timing edit.
